1. Introduction -- where we stand
1.1 Starting point
The UM002B program has completed its pre-silicon Manufacturing Readiness phase (items 16.1­16.4, 17.1­17.2, 18.1­18.3, plus the Phase 4 validation matrix) with a verdict of PASS WITH HONEST FAILURES: coherence audit 15/15 clean, TORR v3 at 8 closed / 9 partial / 7 requires-fab, and the sign-off subset at 0 closed / 5 partial / 2 requires-fab. Everything below builds on that baseline -- nothing here re-litigates it.
The modeled design point, for reference:
· Cell: 2T1C1M. T_DRAM (thin-oxide) + capacitor forms the volatile DRAM path (~2.8 ns access, refresh-maintained). T_NVM (thick-oxide, ~7 nm modeled) + HfO memristor forms the nonvolatile shadow (data as resistance, retained without power).
· Modes: NORMAL (all host traffic on the DRAM path; memristor idle, off the critical path), SAVE (power-fail event: charge  resistance, row by row, powered by onboard reserve capacitors), OFF (zero standby energy), RESTORE (power-up: resistance  charge, with ECC).
· Controller: backup/restore sequencing, power-fail detection, ECC, test access, and retained metadata live in the on-die array periphery.
· Characterization basis: backup energy and timing (~34 µs, ~12.5 µJ) were established on the modeled 68×148 array only and must not be extrapolated to product-scale arrays.
1.2 Open device issues to be closed on this roadmap
Three issues are on record as honest failures. Each is re-stated here with its current numbers, because the roadmap's job is to close them -- not to redescribe them optimistically.
1. Cold-corner RESET misses REQ-006 (30 ns) at -40 °C. Real-device (SPICEclass) simulation shows 0.00× margin against the 1.5× target -- it fails even at 3.3 V with 8× drive strength. Known recovery paths: program voltage of 4.06­4.48 V (outside the 3.3 V domain, implying charge-pump circuitry) or a device-level voltage-acceleration factor of   0.40 eV/V (projected 8.44 ns RESET -- projection only, pending measured data).

2. Shared-bitline disturb fails at 85 °C. Passes cold, marginal at nominal, fails hot. Modeled safe window 2.0­4.45 V at nominal; the disturb threshold sags to ~4.07 V (assumed analytical value) at 85 °C, collapsing the window. All figures are modeled under assumed parameters -- not silicon proof.
3. Far-row IR drop 80­98 mV vs 75 mV budget at nominal/hot. SET remains functional, but margin is consumed. 1.3 What the PDK changes, and what the JDA changes
The PDK is descriptive. It supplies silicon-calibrated transistor models, real design rules, the real metal stack, and the foundry's sign-off decks. With it, every simulation, layout, and margin in the program is re-baselined against fab truth. It fixes the designclosable portion of the three issues: driver sizing, biasing schemes, wire sizing, pitch, grid. The JDA is prescriptive. The HfO stack's switching kinetics, voltage acceleration, disturb threshold, and retention are properties of materials and factory recipes -- they cannot be changed in a layout editor. The JDA buys wafer splits, measurement, model extraction, and iteration with the foundry until the device meets its targets. It fixes the material portion. For 100% of the custom-device vision, there is no substitute; the alternative (a node with qualified embedded ReRAM, PDK-only) is a different program and out of scope here. The two tracks run in parallel wherever possible: Phase 1 (PDK hardening) and Phase 2 (JDA device development) overlap, and both feed Phase 3 (full-chip build).

Objective: re-baseline the entire design against fab truth; close everything closable in design; quantify the remainder and hand it to the JDA track with numbers, not adjectives.

2.1 PDK install and flow setup
· Acquire the PDK under foundry NDA. Pin the PDK version and place it under version control; document NDA and export-control handling -- the PDK is the foundry's confidential property and the program's legal footing starts here.
· Assemble the design flow from PDK-delivered decks: SPICE-class simulator with calibrated compact models, schematic/layout capture, parasitic extraction (PEX) deck, and DRC/LVS decks. Prefer no-cost tooling wherever the PDK supports it; the foundry's sign-off decks are mandatory regardless of tool choice.
· Regression first: re-run the existing netlists unmodified against the calibrated models before changing anything. This isolates model deltas from design deltas -- if a result moves, you know whether the model or your edit moved it.
2.2 Cell and array re-layout to real rules
· Port the 2T1C1M cell to the real DRC rule set. Select the T_NVM thick-oxide device from the PDK's IO device offerings (verify its voltage tolerance against the program-voltage range under evaluation, including the 4.06­4.48 V recovery option).
· Validate the 132×288 nm pitch recommendation against real rules -- confirm or revise with written justification. The buried-wordline experiment's negative result stands; do not re-spend effort there without new evidence.
· Deliverable: a DRC/LVS-clean array tile, with a rule-waiver log (every waiver justified in writing, none silent).
2.3 PEX-based parasitic re-extraction
· Extract with the real metal stack: bitline RC, inter-wire coupling capacitance (the disturb analysis input), and wire resistance (the IR-drop input).
· Back-annotate into the simulation benches and archive the extraction corners alongside the schematics. From this point, no bench runs on assumed parasitics.
2.4 Corner re-simulation
· Full matrix: -40 / 25 / 85 °C × SS / TT / FF, plus Monte Carlo on the sense and ECC paths for mismatch.
· Re-run the three issue benches with calibrated transistor models: · RESET timing at -40 °C against REQ-006 (30 ns, 1.5× margin target);

· disturb window at 85 °C against the (still assumed, pending JDA measurement) threshold;
· IR drop at nominal/hot on the extracted grid against the 75 mV budget. · Output: a quantified gap-to-target per issue, in numbers. "Marginal" is retired as a
status -- every issue gets a value and a margin.
2.5 Per-issue design actions
· Cold RESET: upsize the T_NVM driver for cold-corner drive strength using calibrated models. In parallel, evaluate the charge-pump option for the 4.06­4.48 V domain -- cost it honestly (level shifters, sequencing, area, standby leakage) before committing. Device- improvement stays on the JDA track; do not burn design margin chasing a device-physics problem with transistors.
· Disturb: evaluate biasing-scheme variants and bitline segmentation using PDKavailable devices. Requirement: worst-case unselected-cell bias stays below the disturb threshold with explicit margin at 85 °C. If no biasing scheme closes it with the assumed threshold, that is a finding, not a failure -- it sets the JDA's disturbthreshold target (§3.1).
· IR drop: power-grid strap widening, grid planning, and pitch finalization against the real sheet resistance. Close the 75 mV budget on the extracted grid. Carry forward HS-5, IR-5, HS-1 at 6.8 µs, and the 20 ns thermal pulse ceiling; re-validate each against the real stack.
2.6 Entry / exit criteria
· Entry: PDK installed, version-pinned, NDA documented; prior netlists under version control; per-issue success metrics written down before any editing begins.
· Exit: DRC/LVS-clean array tile; corner simulations complete with calibrated models; each open issue re-baselined with a number and explicitly partitioned into design-closable vs JDA-required. Nothing proceeds to Phase 3 on assumed models.
3. Phase 2 -- JDA device development
Objective: make the HfO device real -- measured on wafers, modeled from data, frozen as a characterized device in the private flow.

3.1 Device target agreement
Before any wafer runs, agree the targets with the foundry in writing, in numbers:
· Switching kinetics:   0.40 eV/V (projected 8.44 ns RESET at target drive -- labeled as projection until measured).
· Disturb threshold at 85 °C: must clear the worst-case unselected bias selected in §2.5 with explicit margin. The numeric value is set by the Phase 1 biasing choice -- the two phases handshake here.
· Retention: 10-year at 85 °C as the design target; the bake methodology (acceleration model, sample sizes, read criteria) is agreed now, and the proof is collected in Phase 4. Target defined today, evidence later -- never conflated.
3.2 Test-chip planning
· This is a device test chip, not the product: 1T1R test arrays (statistical switching distributions across hundreds of devices -- no hero-device reporting), Kelvin / 4point structures (isolate filament resistance from series parasitics), and processcontrol monitors on every split.
· Stack splits: HfO thickness, electrode material/workfunction, oxygen stoichiometry, post-deposition anneal conditions. Each split is a hypothesis about , disturb immunity, or retention -- logged as such.
· Tester requirements: temperature chuck covering -40 °C to 125 °C, fast pulsing (sub-30 ns resolution) for RESET kinetics, and low-current sense for disturb detection.
· Secure the JDA shuttle slot and plan sample counts for statistics, not anecdotes.
3.3 Measurement and Verilog-A extraction
· Measure switching kinetics vs voltage and temperature  extract  and thermal activation energy. Measure disturb threshold vs temperature. Run the agreed retention bake.
· Fit the Verilog-A compact model to the measured data. Then QA the model against the data across the full matrix -- typical and corners. A model that fits only typical is a liability; reject it.
· Deliverable: extracted model + extraction report with residuals, so any engineer can see where the model is trustworthy and where it is thin.

3.4 Iteration spins
· Plan for multiple spins; a single-spin success is the exception in device R&D, not the plan.
· Define per-spin go/no-go against the §3.1 targets before the wafers run. Record negative results with the same care as positive ones -- a split that fails cleanly teaches the next split.
3.5 Stack freeze
· Freeze the stack recipe, freeze the Verilog-A model, freeze the ReRAM-module design rules. From here the device is treated like a PDK device: characterized, versioned, and trusted within its qualified envelope.
· Hand back to design: re-run the Phase 1 benches with the frozen model and confirm the closure numbers. The loop between Phase 1 and Phase 2 closes here.
3.6 Entry / exit criteria
· Entry: JDA signed; §3.1 targets agreed in writing; test-chip shuttle slot secured; tester capability confirmed.
· Exit: stack frozen; model extracted and QA'd; all three device targets demonstrated on measured silicon -- or a documented waiver with a recovery plan. No waiver is silent.
4. Phase 3 -- Full-chip build
Objective: integrate the hardened array tile, the verified controller, and the frozen device model into a signed-off, tapeout-ready chip. 4.1 Integration
· Array: tile  full array with row/column decoding, sense amplifiers, and write drivers sized per the §2.5 outcomes.
· Controller RTL: power-fail detection, SAVE/RESTORE sequencing, ECC encode/ decode, test access ports, and retained-metadata register file. The RTL must be functionally verified (constrained-random + directed tests, including power-fail injection at arbitrary points in a SAVE sequence) before it touches layout.

· IO ring: pad ring with ESD protection per the foundry's ESD rules; signal integrity on high-speed interfaces validated against the PDK's IO models.
· Top-level power grid: designed to the IR-drop budget closed in §2.5, verified on the extracted full-chip grid -- not the tile grid.
· Clock and reset distribution: skew and integrity checked; the power-fail detector's own supply domain reviewed (it must stay alive long enough to launch SAVE -- its hold-up is a design requirement, not an assumption).
4.2 Physical implementation
· Full-chip DRC/LVS with the foundry decks; zero violations or a written waiver log. · Full-chip PEX. Re-check: bitline RC at array scale, grid resistance at chip scale,
coupling on long routes.
4.3 Sign-off
· STA across all corners (-40/25/85 °C × SS/TT/FF), including the SAVE/RESTORE sequencing paths -- the emergency path gets the same timing rigor as the functional path.
· Power integrity: IR-drop and electromigration sign-off with the foundry's decks on the extracted grid.
· Reliability checks per foundry requirements (electromigration and other required mechanisms -- use the foundry's checklist, not a generic one).
· Issue re-verification at full-chip level: all three open issues re-run with extracted full-chip parasitics and the frozen JDA device model. An issue is closed only when it passes here -- tile-level closure is necessary but not sufficient.
4.4 DFT and test modes
· Array BIST for manufacturing test; scan/ATPG per foundry requirements. · SAVE/RESTORE test modes: forced power-fail injection with snapshot verify, so
the tester -- and later the qualification lab -- can exercise the backup path deterministically rather than hoping a real power cut arrives on schedule.
4.5 Tapeout
· First tapeout goes on an MPW (multi-project wafer) shuttle: mask costs shared across projects, which keeps first silicon affordable. Accept the trade -- fewer samples, shuttle-schedule timing -- with eyes open.

· The bring-up board and the tester program are ready before silicon returns. Silicon waiting on a test board is schedule burned for nothing.
4.6 Entry / exit criteria
· Entry: frozen device model in the flow; DRC/LVS-clean tiles; controller RTL functionally verified including power-fail injection; written test plan.
· Exit: signed-off GDSII taped out on MPW; bring-up hardware and tester program ready.
5. Phase 4 -- Silicon validation
Objective: replace every remaining "modeled" and "target" in the evidence ledger with measured data -- or write down exactly where reality diverged and what happens next.
5.1 Bring-up
· Power-on, controller communication, then the basic SAVE  OFF  RESTORE loopback on the tester using the §4.4 test modes.
· Shmoo the program voltages: find the real operating window on silicon and compare it against the modeled 2.0­4.45 V window. Record the delta -- it calibrates every model downstream.
5.2 Issue-mapped characterization
Each open issue gets a dedicated measurement campaign, mapped one-to-one:
· Cold RESET: temperature chamber at -40 °C; measure the RESET time distribution across many cells and dies. Pass criterion: REQ-006 (30 ns) with the 1.5× margin target, on statistics -- not on the best cell.
· Disturb: 85 °C hammer testing -- repeatedly write selected cells while monitoring unselected neighbors for resistance drift. Compare against the threshold agreed in §3.1.
· Retention: accelerated high-temperature bake with Arrhenius extrapolation toward the 10-year/85 °C design target, using the methodology agreed with the foundry in §3.1. This is industry-standard inference, and it is labeled as inference -- the honest statement is "bake data extrapolates to X," never "ten years measured."

5.3 Debug and re-spin criteria
Decide the rules before silicon arrives, while heads are cool:
· Functional failure (controller bug, sequencing error, dead test mode)  rootcause, then ECO/metal-layer fix if possible, else full re-spin.
· Parametric miss (RESET too slow, disturb too high, drop too large)  classify as design-fixable (re-spin with a design change from the Phase 1 playbook) or devicelimited (back to the JDA track for another stack iteration). The classification, not the disappointment, drives the next step.
5.4 Qualification and the honest claim ladder
· Run the foundry's required JEDEC-style qualification flow (high-temperature operating life and related stresses, per the qualification checklist -- described here generically because the exact flow is the foundry's to specify). Qualification is what stands between "characterized" and "production-worthy."
· The claim ladder -- the discipline this whole program has followed: · After Phase 4 characterization: internal claims only ("RESET measured at X ns at
-40 °C across N cells/dies"). · After qualification: product claims (temperature rating, retention) may be stated
externally. · No claim moves up a rung without the data behind it. The ledger from the pre-
silicon program (proven / modeled / target / requires-fab) stays live through silicon -- rows get promoted on evidence, never on optimism.
6. Parallel software track
The hardware program above does not block the software program, and the software program must not become an excuse to delay hardware decisions.
· OS persistent-memory enablement and the shutdown/resume firmware (graceful-shutdown orchestration, clean-shutdown flag, hibernation-style resume) are developed in parallel on emulators and FPGA models of the array -- no silicon required.
· Freeze the host interface spec early (DDR-style / NVDIMM-N-like command and timing interface). The interface is the contract between the two tracks; once frozen,

7. Residual risks register
Short, honest, and kept live -- a risk not written down is a risk not managed. 1. JDA partner and schedule. A JDA is negotiated, not purchased; foundries choose
their partners and set their pace. Mitigation: begin engagement early, keep the PDK-only fallback (§1.3) as a live option. 2. Device target achievability.   0.40 eV/V, 85 °C disturb immunity, and the retention target are goals, not entitlements. Mitigation: multi-spin budget in the plan; per-spin go/no-go (§3.4). 3. Retention extrapolation. Accelerated bake is inference, however standard. Mitigation: label it as such; widen sample sizes; never let marketing outrun the bake data. 4. First-silicon bugs. Expect a re-spin; the industry norm, not a failure. Mitigation: MPW keeps the cost survivable; §5.3 rules decided in advance. 5. Funding phase-gates. JDA fees, MPW runs, and qualification each need capital. Mitigation: align financing milestones with the phase exits defined above -- each exit is a fundable proof point. 6. Model risk. Every simulation is only as good as its models. Mitigation: the measured-data checkpoints (§3.3 extraction QA, §5.1 shmoo, §5.2 characterization) are the backstops; no model survives contact with contradicting silicon.
